NARRATION LISTENING CONTRACT — TWO FILLED FICTIONAL EXAMPLES
Status: local companion asset; not publication or destination evidence
Author: Alfred, an AI assistant

Purpose
-------
These explicitly fictional examples exercise the copyable narration listening
contract. They show why an approved script, a clean solo voice track, correct
captions, or a passing editor-timeline review cannot approve different mix or
delivery bytes.

Every release identifier, voice source, timecode, observation, and outcome below
is invented for the examples. No real narrator, person, account, customer,
upload, destination, publication, audience, metric, revenue, accessibility
result, or platform outcome is described.

Evidence states
---------------
PASS        The declared check passed for the exact identity and scope.
FAIL        A release-blocking mismatch or listening defect was observed.
BLOCKED     A required source, authority, surface, or prerequisite is absent.
NOT_TESTED  The declared check was not performed.
UNCERTAIN   Available evidence does not support either PASS or FAIL.
SUPERSEDED  Historical evidence does not approve the current revision.

Neither example authorizes upload, publication, promotion, contact, or outreach.

CROSS-EXAMPLE REVIEW
==========================================================================

Example 1 demonstrates a composition failure: the voice source and isolated edit
are correct, but an effect masks a meaning pivot in the mix and delivery.

Example 2 demonstrates an identity failure: the current edit and admitted mix are
correct, but delivery contains a superseded quantity while captions and picture
carry the replacement.

Together they show five release rules:

1. Script approval is text evidence, not listening evidence.
2. Voice-origin authority is separate from audible quality and meaning.
3. A solo-voice pass cannot approve a complete mix.
4. An editor or mix pass cannot approve separately identified delivery bytes.
5. Correct captions or picture cannot compensate for contradictory narration.

The examples preserve PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED, NOT_TESTED, UNCERTAIN, and SUPERSEDED
as non-interchangeable states. They keep destination rendition NOT_OBSERVED,
publication NOT_PUBLIC, and all outcomes within synthetic local method scope.
