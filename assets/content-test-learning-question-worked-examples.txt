CONTENT TEST LEARNING QUESTION — WORKED SYNTHETIC EXAMPLES
==========================================================

Original worked examples by Alfred.

Purpose
-------
These filled traces demonstrate how to use one learning question without turning
release readiness into audience evidence. Both examples are fictional. They
represent no real platform, account, channel, upload, post, audience, customer,
metric, conversion, revenue, or result.

The examples test the decision method, not a destination. They do not prove that
a candidate is safe, that a release occurred, that a metric is available, or
that one creative treatment performs better than another.

Truthful evidence vocabulary
----------------------------
PASS         Identified evidence satisfies the declared check.
FAIL         Identified evidence contradicts the declared check.
BLOCKED      Required evidence is missing, inaccessible, stale, or conflicting.
NOT_TESTED   The check has not been performed.
SUPERSEDED   A changed candidate, question, destination, or rule invalidated it.
INSUFFICIENT The observation window closed without decision-grade evidence.
CONFLICTED   A material confounder or opposing evidence prevents interpretation.
STOP         A truth, rights, privacy, safety, policy, or quality gate failed.

A deliberately induced failure can produce a PASS for the review method when the
observed decision matches the predeclared safe response. It is not evidence that
a real release system passed.


EXAMPLE 1 — NO VERIFIED DESTINATION, SO NO CONTENT INFERENCE
============================================================

Synthetic condition
-------------------
Two locally valid short-form candidates differ only in their opening treatment.
The release path has no verified owned destination. The expected decision is
BLOCKED before upload, with every audience-observation lane left NOT_TESTED.

A. Test envelope
----------------
Worksheet identity: synthetic-learning-trace-01
Prepared by role: synthetic reviewer
Owned destination under review: placeholder destination D
Destination ownership evidence: unavailable
Account or channel state: NOT VERIFIED
Topic boundary: a generic product-friction explanation
Intended audience need: understand one concrete symptom and one smallest fix

Exact editorial decision:
Decide whether a concrete user-cost opening should be tested against a
pattern-label opening for the next eligible release on one owned destination.

Out of scope:
- cross-destination comparison
- total displayed-view optimization
- conversion, follower, customer, or revenue conclusions
- changing the body claim, rights basis, disclosure, captions, or visual system

Predeclared stop condition:
If destination ownership and an allowed release path cannot be verified, stop
before upload and keep both candidates local.

Test-envelope result: BLOCKED
Evidence: no identified ownership record or verified destination state
Reason: destination eligibility is required before any release transition

B. One learning question
------------------------
Learning question:
For the next eligible short on placeholder destination D, does opening with the
concrete user cost support a stronger first-segment hold than opening with the
pattern label, while the body, duration, visuals, audio, captions, topic, and
release protocol remain materially equivalent?

Editorial decision: whether to repeat the concrete-cost opening treatment
Eligible release population: two exact synthetic candidates on destination D
Single interpretable surface: opening treatment
Primary observation: a destination-defined first-segment hold measure
Evidence window: begins only after logged-out public verification and closes at
the predeclared destination reporting boundary

Learning-question result: BLOCKED
Reason: the question is structurally narrow, but its destination and authoritative
primary observation are not currently verified

C. Candidate identities and states
-----------------------------------
Candidate A ID: synthetic-hook-label-a
Master identity: synthetic digest A-001
Opening: “This looks like a checkout-state bug.”
Current state: LOCAL
State evidence: exact local candidate record only

Candidate B ID: synthetic-hook-cost-b
Master identity: synthetic digest B-001
Opening: “A buyer can pay and still be unsure the order exists.”
Current state: LOCAL
State evidence: exact local candidate record only

Candidate-identity result: PASS
Reason: the synthetic records distinguish the candidates and preserve LOCAL as
separate from PRIVATE, QUEUED, PUBLIC, and PUBLICLY_VERIFIED

D. Equal release eligibility
-----------------------------
Candidate A truth and claim boundary: PASS in the synthetic record
Candidate A rights and provenance: PASS in the synthetic record
Candidate A privacy and identity: PASS in the synthetic record
Candidate A disclosure and accessibility: PASS in the synthetic record
Candidate A provider-processed review: NOT_TESTED
Candidate A overall eligibility: BLOCKED

Candidate B truth and claim boundary: PASS in the synthetic record

Local editorial checks cannot supply destination ownership, upload, processing,

-----------------------------------

Variant A treatment: pattern label first
Variant B treatment: concrete user cost first

F. Release ladder
-----------------
1. Local eligibility: PASS for the identified synthetic artifacts only

G. Observation and decision
----------------------------
Primary destination metric label: unavailable
Authoritative definition: unavailable
Destination value: MISSING
Missing-value treatment: MISSING, not zero
Audience interpretation permitted: none

Decision: BLOCKED
Reason: no verified destination, public release, observation window, or audience
evidence exists
Narrow action permitted: resolve destination ownership and eligibility before
re-running the release review
Actions not permitted: call either hook a winner, call either hook an

Why this trace is useful
------------------------
The candidates can be locally ready while the content test remains blocked. The
trace prevents a local validation result from becoming an upload claim and
prevents a blocked release from becoming a performance conclusion.


EXAMPLE 2 — METRIC DEFINITION CHANGES DURING THE WINDOW
=======================================================

Synthetic condition
-------------------
Two candidates are assumed, only for this fictional trace, to have completed
sequential verified releases on the same placeholder destination. During the
second observation window, the destination changes the published definition of
the primary metric. The expected decision is SUPERSEDED or CONFLICTED rather
than ADOPT or RETAIN.

This example does not assert that any real destination offers this metric or
that any real release occurred.

A. Test envelope
----------------
Worksheet identity: synthetic-learning-trace-02
Prepared by role: synthetic reviewer
Owned destination under review: placeholder destination Q
Destination state: assumed verified only inside this fictional record
Topic boundary: one generic onboarding-friction explanation

Exact editorial decision:
Decide whether to repeat a concrete user-cost opening on a comparable next topic.

Single interpretable surface: first spoken and captioned opening treatment
Controlled surfaces:
- same bounded topic and factual basis
- materially equivalent body and duration
- same original visual system
- equivalent narration intelligibility
- complete reviewed captions
- same destination and public visibility
- same release and verification procedure

Primary observation:
“first-segment hold,” using destination definition revision m1

Evidence-window rule:
Open after verified release; close after the declared reporting delay; reject a
comparison if the metric definition or eligibility population changes before
both candidate windows are reconciled.

Test-envelope result: PASS for the frozen synthetic design

B. Candidates and release order
-------------------------------
Candidate A ID: synthetic-pattern-a
Master identity: synthetic digest A-101
Opening treatment: pattern label first
State in this fictional trace: PUBLICLY_VERIFIED

Candidate B ID: synthetic-cost-b
Master identity: synthetic digest B-101
Opening treatment: concrete user cost first
State in this fictional trace: PUBLICLY_VERIFIED

Release order:
1. A completes its declared window under metric definition m1.
2. B releases later under the same planned protocol.
3. Before B’s window closes, the destination marks definition m2 current.

Both release identities and routes are placeholders. They are not usable URLs or
publication evidence.

C. Predeclared comparison rule
------------------------------
Eligible comparison requires:
- both exact candidates publicly verified on the same destination;
- both windows complete;
- the same metric label, definition, eligibility population, and reporting
  status across both observations;
- no material moderation, delivery, processing, or visibility anomaly;
- no post-release candidate change; and
- enough evidence for the declared narrow decision.

If the metric definition changes during either window:
- mark the original comparison SUPERSEDED if values cannot be recomputed under
  one common current definition;
- mark it CONFLICTED if old and new scopes coexist without reliable separation;
- do not substitute total displayed views after observing the results;
- do not label either treatment a winner or loser.

Observation-plan result before release: PASS

D. Synthetic observation record
--------------------------------
Candidate A:
Released identity: synthetic digest A-101
Public verification: PASS inside this fictional trace
Metric label: first-segment hold
Definition revision: m1
Window state: CLOSED
Displayed value: illustrative value withheld
Evidence state: SUPERSEDED for the intended A-versus-B comparison
Reason: B cannot be reconciled under the same known metric definition

Candidate B:
Released identity: synthetic digest B-101
Public verification: PASS inside this fictional trace
Metric label: first-segment hold
Definition revision at window open: m1
Definition revision at review: m2
Window state: CLOSED
Displayed value: illustrative value withheld
Evidence state: CONFLICTED
Reason: the record cannot establish which definition and population shaped the
window’s reported value

Cross-candidate observation integrity:
Candidate identity continuity: PASS
Same destination: PASS
Same intended creative surface: PASS
Same metric label: PASS
Same metric definition: FAIL
Same eligibility population: BLOCKED
Missing values kept distinct from zero: PASS
Post-hoc substitute metric used: NO

E. Decision record
------------------
Decision: SUPERSEDED
Reason: the predeclared comparison required one common metric definition, but
the synthetic observation record contains m1 and m2 without a trustworthy
common reconciliation

Narrow action permitted:
Design a new comparison only after confirming a stable current definition and
recording a new question, candidate set, and evidence window.

Actions not permitted:
- ADOPT the concrete-cost treatment
- RETAIN the pattern-label treatment based on this comparison
- claim platform causality
- compare withheld illustrative values
- replace the primary metric with displayed views after observation
- merge values across destinations
- claim improved reach, conversion, customers, or revenue

Method-check result: PASS
Reason: the review preserved the definition change, rejected a post-hoc metric
swap, and selected the predeclared uncertainty state instead of manufacturing a
winner

Why this trace is useful
------------------------
Public availability and completed windows are not enough. Interpretation also
depends on stable definitions and comparable populations. A changed definition
can preserve each historical observation while invalidating the planned
comparison. SUPERSEDED is therefore a useful result, not missing polish.


COMPACT REVIEW OF BOTH EXAMPLES
===============================

1. One exact editorial decision is named: PASS
2. One interpretable surface is allowed to vary: PASS
3. Controlled surfaces and dependent edits are explicit: PASS
4. Equal safety and quality gates remain mandatory: PASS
5. Candidate states are not inferred from local validation: PASS
6. Local, public, and publicly verified remain distinct: PASS
7. Cross-platform displayed metrics are not merged: PASS
8. Primary observations are selected before release: PASS
9. Missing values remain missing rather than zero: PASS
10. Blocked release is not described as underperformance: PASS
11. Definition change invalidates rather than improves the comparison: PASS
12. No post-hoc substitute metric is allowed: PASS
13. BLOCKED, CONFLICTED, and SUPERSEDED remain available: PASS
14. Conclusions remain narrower than the synthetic evidence: PASS
15. No safety, truth, rights, privacy, disclosure, accessibility, or quality
    constraint becomes a test variant: PASS

Rights and scope
----------------
These examples, candidate labels, placeholder digests, questions, observations,
and decisions are original explanatory material by Alfred.
They use no third-party media, private account data, personal attribution,
customer material, destination screenshot, copied metric value, or real result.

The examples are filled method demonstrations, not test certificates. Before a
real release, current first-party destination rules, ownership, metric
definitions, rights, privacy, disclosure, accessibility, processing, visibility,
and public-verification requirements must be checked with identified evidence.
